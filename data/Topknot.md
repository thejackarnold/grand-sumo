A sumo rookie doesn't "receive" his topknot at a specific ceremony or rank; rather, it is a rite of passage dictated almost entirely by **hair length**.

Since professional sumo wrestlers are required to grow their hair to maintain the tradition of the samurai, the timeline for a rookie depends on how fast their hair grows and how quickly they climb the ranks.

## 1. The Growth Stages
A wrestler's hairstyle evolves as they progress:
- **Zambara (Loose/Wild):** When a rookie first enters a stable (often with a buzzcut or short hair), they simply let it grow. Until it is long enough to tie up, the hair is left loose or roughly combed. This "shaggy" look is called _zambara_.
- **Chonmage (Standard Topknot):** This is the basic topknot worn by most wrestlers in the lower four divisions (_Jonokuchi_ through _Makushita_) and by all wrestlers during training. A rookie "receives" his first _chonmage_ as soon as his hair is long enough to be oiled and tied—usually after about **one year** of growth (roughly shoulder length).
- **Oichomage (Ginkgo-Leaf Style):** This is the elaborate, fan-shaped topknot you see on TV. It is strictly reserved for **Sekitori** (the top two salaried divisions).

---

## 2. The "Fast-Track" Exception
Occasionally, a "monster rookie" (a highly successful amateur who starts at a higher rank or wins every match) will rise through the divisions so fast that his hair cannot keep up.

In these rare cases, you might see a wrestler competing in the top _Makuuchi_ division against elite champions while still wearing his hair in the "rookie" _zambara_ style or a very short _chonmage_. This is often seen as a mark of a true prodigy, as their skill has literally outpaced their biology.

---

## 3. The Tokoyama (The Hairdressers)
The topknot is not something the wrestler does himself. Each stable has (or shares) a **Tokoyama**—a specialist hairdresser trained in the ancient art of sumo hairstyling.
- **The Kit:** They use _bintsuke-abura_ (a thick, sweet-smelling chamomile wax) and traditional wooden combs.
- **The Daily Ritual:** A wrestler’s hair is styled every morning. For lower-ranked rookies, this is often their first lesson in the patience and discipline required of a _rikishi_.

## 4. The Retirement Ceremony (_Danpatsu-shiki_)
Because the topknot is so tied to a wrestler's identity, the only time it is "taken away" is at the end of their career. In a formal ceremony at the Ryogoku Kokugikan, hundreds of people (stablemates, sponsors, and family) take turns cutting a single strand of the hair until the stablemaster makes the final snip, signifying the wrestler's return to "civilian" life.
