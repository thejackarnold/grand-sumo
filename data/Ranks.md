Professional sumo wrestling is governed by a strict, hierarchical ranking system called the **Banzuke**. This system is updated after each of the six annual tournaments (_honbasho_) based on a wrestler's win-loss record.

The ranking is divided into six main divisions. The top two are the "salaried" professional ranks (**Sekitori**), while the bottom four are considered trainees.

---

|**Division**|**Wrestlers**|**Matches per Tournament**|**Status**|
|---|---|---|---|

|**Juryo**|28|15 (Daily)|Second Division; professional status|

|**Sandanme**|~200|7 (Every other day)|Junior/Apprentice|

|**Jonokuchi**|~50|7 (Every other day)|Entry-level division|

---

## 2. Ranks within the Top Division (Makuuchi)
Within the top division, wrestlers are further ranked by title. These are known as the **Sanyaku** (titleholders) and **Maegashira**.
- **Yokozuna (Grand Champion):** The pinnacle of sumo. Promotion requires two consecutive tournament championships (_yusho_) or an "equivalent performance" as an Ozeki. **Yokozuna cannot be demoted.** If their performance drops, they are expected to retire.
- **Ozeki (Champion):** The second-highest rank. To reach Ozeki, a wrestler usually needs **33 wins over three consecutive tournaments** while ranked as a Sekiwake or Komusubi. Demotion occurs if an Ozeki has two consecutive losing records (_make-koshi_).
- **Sekiwake & Komusubi:** The "junior champion" ranks. There must be at least two of each (one East, one West) in every tournament.
- **Maegashira:** The bulk of the Makuuchi division, numbered from #1 (highest) down to ~#17.

---

## 3. General Promotion & Demotion Rules
Outside of the Yokozuna and Ozeki ranks, the rules are primarily mathematical but subject to the Japan Sumo Association's (JSA) final decision.
- **Kachi-koshi (Winning Record):** Getting a majority of wins (8+ in Makuuchi/Juryo, 4+ in lower divisions). This almost always results in a promotion.
- **Make-koshi (Losing Record):** Getting a majority of losses. This typically results in a demotion.
- **East vs. West:** Each rank is split into East and West. **East is slightly more prestigious.** An East-ranked wrestler who goes 8-7 might only move up to the West side of the next higher rank.

---

## 4. Entry Requirements
To enter professional sumo, recruits must join a stable (_heya_) and meet these physical and age standards:
- **Age:** Between 15 and 23 years old (can be extended to 25 for successful amateur/college wrestlers).
- **Height:** Minimum 167 cm (approx. 5'6").
- **Weight:** Minimum 67 kg (approx. 148 lbs).
- **Gender:** Only men are permitted in professional sumo.

---

## 5. Special Stats & Awards
For those tracking "sumo stats," three special prizes (**Sansho**) are awarded to Makuuchi wrestlers (below Ozeki) who achieve a winning record:
1. **Shukun-sho (Outstanding Performance):** Usually for defeating the Yokozuna or tournament winner.
2. **Kanto-sho (Fighting Spirit):** For displaying exceptional drive or resilience.
3. **Gino-sho (Technique):** For using a wide variety or high level of traditional techniques.

> **Note on Kinboshi:** A "Gold Star" (_kinboshi_) is a specific stat awarded to a Maegashira-ranked wrestler who defeats a Yokozuna. Each star provides a permanent salary bonus for the rest of their career.
