The special prizes, known as **Sansho** (literally "Three Prizes"), are the most prestigious individual awards in professional sumo outside of winning the tournament championship (_yusho_). They were introduced in 1947 to recognize exceptional performance among wrestlers who are not yet at the very top of the hierarchy.

---

## 1. The Three Categories
Each prize recognizes a different aspect of a wrestler's performance during the 15-day tournament:
- **Shukun-sho (Outstanding Performance Prize):** Awarded for significant achievements, most commonly for **defeating the Yokozuna** or the eventual tournament winner. It is also given to wrestlers who have a high impact on the tournament's final outcome relative to their rank.
- **Kanto-sho (Fighting Spirit Prize):** Awarded for tenacity and "guts." It frequently goes to **top-division debutants** who achieve double-digit wins (10+), or to veteran wrestlers who show incredible resilience despite injury or difficult matchups.
- **Gino-sho (Technique Prize):** Considered the most prestigious (and most often withheld), this is awarded to wrestlers who demonstrate a **wide variety of technical moves** (_kimarite_) or exceptional skill in traditional sumo fundamentals. Judges look for variety beyond the standard push-outs and force-outs.
    

---

## 2. Eligibility & Requirements
Not every wrestler can win a Sansho. There are two "hard" rules for eligibility:
1. **Rank Cap:** Only wrestlers in the **Makuuchi** (top) division ranked **Sekiwake or below** are eligible. Once a wrestler reaches the rank of **Ozeki** or **Yokozuna**, they are no longer eligible for these prizes (as they are expected to perform at an elite level by default).
2. **Kachi-koshi (Winning Record):** A wrestler **must** achieve a winning record (at least 8–7) to receive a prize. Even if a wrestler defeats every Yokozuna in the tournament, they cannot receive the prize if they finish with a losing record (_make-koshi_).

---

## 3. Financial & Physical Rewards
For a wrestler building their "sumo stats," these prizes are highly lucrative:
- **Cash Prize:** Each Sansho comes with a bonus of **2,000,000 yen** (approx. **$13,000–$14,000 USD**).
- **The Trophies:** Winners receive a large, elaborate plaque and a specific silver trophy depicting a wrestler holding a samurai helmet (_kabuto_).
- **Salary Impact:** While not a permanent salary bump like a _kinboshi_ (Gold Star), winning multiple prizes is a key factor the Japan Sumo Association uses when deciding who to promote to the _Sanyaku_ (champion) ranks.

---

## 4. The Selection Process
The prizes are decided by a **selection committee** made up of senior judges and members of the press.
- **Timing:** Awards are discussed on the morning of **Day 15** (the final day).
- **Conditional Awards:** Judges often issue "conditional" prizes. For example, a wrestler might be told: _"You will receive the Technique Prize, but only if you win your match today to reach 10 wins."_
- **Multiples:** There is no limit to how many wrestlers can win a specific prize. In July 2023, a record eight total prizes were handed out. Conversely, if no one's performance is deemed worthy, a prize may not be awarded at all.

## Recent 2026 Context
In the most recent **January 2026 (Hatsu Basho)** tournament:
- **Atamifuji** and **Kirishima** both secured Fighting Spirit prizes with strong double-digit performances.
- The **Technique Prize** was notably withheld, as judges felt no eligible wrestler showed enough technical variety to meet the standard.

As of today, **March 18, 2026**, the March tournament (_Haru Basho_) is entering its final stages. With **Sekiwake Takayasu** and **Maegashira Takanosho** currently leading the pack with 6–1 records (as of Day 7), they are the current frontrunners for the Outstanding Performance and Fighting Spirit prizes respectively.
