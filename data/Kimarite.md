In professional sumo, the winning move is called a **Kimarite**. There are currently **82 official winning techniques**, though in a typical tournament, you’ll mostly see the same 10–12 common moves.

When a match ends, the referee (_gyoji_) or the judges (_shimpan_) announce the specific kimarite used. These are broadly categorized into six main groups, plus a seventh category for "non-techniques."

---

## 1. The "Big Three" (Most Common)

If you're building a dashboard for sumo stats, these three usually account for about **60–70%** of all match results.
- **Yorikiri (Frontal Force Out):** The quintessential sumo move. The winner keeps a grip on the opponent's belt (_mawashi_) and simply marches them backward out of the ring.
- **Oshidashi (Frontal Push Out):** Similar to _yorikiri_, but without a belt grip. The wrestler uses his palms to push the opponent’s chest or shoulders until they step out.
- **Hatakikomi (Slap Down):** A favorite of smaller, agile wrestlers. As the opponent charges, the winner sidesteps and slaps them on the back or shoulders, using their own momentum to face-plant them into the clay.

---

## 2. Throwing Techniques (_Nagewaza_)
These are the most spectacular moves, often involving high-leverage pivots.
- **Uwatenage (Overarm Throw):** Grabbing the opponent's belt from the _outside_ of their arm and swinging them to the ground.
- **Shitatenage (Underarm Throw):** Grabbing the belt from _inside_ (underneath) the opponent’s arm to execute the throw.
- **Sukuinage (Beltless Arm Throw):** A powerful throw where the wrestler doesn't even use the belt; they just hook their arm under the opponent's armpit and heave.

---

## 3. Leg Tripping & Twisting (_Kakewaza & Hineriwaza_)
- **Sotogake (Outside Leg Trip):** Hooking your leg around the _outside_ of the opponent's leg to trip them backward.
- **Uchigake (Inside Leg Trip):** Hooking the _inside_ of the opponent's calf.
- **Katasukashi (Under-shoulder Swing Down):** A technical "twist down" where a wrestler ducks under the opponent's arm, grabs the shoulder, and pulls them down sideways.

---

## 4. Special & Rare Techniques (_Tokushuwaza & Soriwaza_)
These are the "highlight reel" moves that fans go crazy for because they are so rare.
- **Utchari (Backward Pivot Throw):** When a wrestler is about to be pushed out, they lean back over the edge, hook the opponent, and spin them around so the _opponent_ steps out first.
- **Izori (Backwards Body Drop):** A nearly extinct move where a wrestler ducks between the opponent's legs, lifts them onto his back, and falls backward.
- **Tsuridashi (Lift Out):** A pure display of strength where a wrestler picks up their opponent entirely off the ground and carries them out of the ring.

---

## 5. The "Non-Technique" Wins (_Hiwaza_)
Sometimes you win without actually doing a move. These are credited as _Hiwaza_:
- **Isamiashi (Forward Step Out):** You were defending, and your opponent accidentally stepped out of the ring while trying to attack you.
- **Koshikudake (Inadvertent Collapse):** Your opponent simply loses their balance and falls over without you touching them.

---

## 6. Forbidden Moves (_Kinjite_)
Doing any of these results in an immediate disqualification (_hansoku_):
- **Hair pulling** (the most common foul).
- **Striking with a closed fist.**
- **Poking the eyes or solar plexus.**
- **Grabbing the "vertical" part of the mawashi** (the groin area).
- **Choking the throat** (though pushing the throat with an open palm is legal).
